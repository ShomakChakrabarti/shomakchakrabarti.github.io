3.8. Exercises#

3.8.1. Core#

Exercise 3.1 (Core)

Derive (IC) from first principles. Then explain in one sentence each why the required gap \(z_g-z_b\) increases in \(c\) and decreases in \(\Dpi\).

Hint

Write the agent’s expected payoff under each action and subtract.

Exercise 3.2 (Core)

Prove part (a) of Theorem 3.1 for the low action, writing out the perturbation step and the Jensen step in full.

Hint

Copy the structure of the high-action proof; the only change is \(\mu=\mul\) and \(c(\al)=0\).

Exercise 3.3 (Core)

Let \(u(w)=\ln w\), \(\uo=0\), \(\muh=0.8\), \(\mul=0.4\) and \(c=0.2\).

  • (a) Find the first-best wage for the high action.

  • (b) Find the second-best contract \((w_g^*,w_b^*)\) and its expected cost.

  • (c) Compute the agency cost, and check that it is a Jensen gap.

Hint

Here \(h(z)=e^z\). Work in utilities first, then convert.

Exercise 3.4 (Core)

Prove Corollary 3.1. When is the second case strictly positive?

Hint

Total surplus is \(\uo\) plus profit. In the second case, use the first-best condition and the fact that the second best chooses the low action.

Exercise 3.5 (Core)

Use the parameters of Example 3.2, but let \(y_b=200\) and \(y_g=221\).

  • (a) Which action does the principal choose in the first best? In the second best?

  • (b) Compute the welfare loss.

  • (c) Find the full range of \(y_g\) for which the two regimes choose different actions.

Hint

Compare \(\Dpi\Dy\) with \(h(\uo+c)-h(\uo)\) and with \(C^{\SB}-h(\uo)\).

Exercise 3.6 (Core)

Suppose the agent is risk-neutral: \(u(w)=w\). Show that the principal achieves her first-best profit even when the action is hidden. Show that “selling the firm” to the agent at a fixed price is one optimal contract. Which step in the proof of Proposition 3.1 fails?

Hint

With \(h\) linear the expected wage bill depends only on mean utility.

Exercise 3.7 (Core)

With \(u(w)=\sqrt w\), show that the agency cost \(AC\) in (3.6) is strictly decreasing in \(\muh\) when \(\mul\) is held fixed. Interpret.

Hint

Differentiate \(g(\muh)=\muh(1-\muh)/(\muh-\mul)^2\). The sign of the numerator simplifies to \(-[\muh(1-\mul)+\mul(1-\muh)]\).

Exercise 3.8 (Core)

Suppose \(\muh=\mul\). Show that no contract induces the high action, and explain why this does not contradict Theorem 3.2.

Hint

Look at (IC) before dividing by \(\Dpi\).

3.8.2. Cases#

Exercise 3.9 (Case)

In Example 3.2:

  • (a) Under capitation, which constraint fails if the NHS wants proactive care? Show it with the numbers.

  • (b) Under the contract with a core payment and a bonus, compute the GP’s expected utility from each action. Why does (IC) bind while (PC) is slack?

  • (c) Give two reasons, outside the model, why the NHS did not cut the core payment to \(93.12\) to save \(7.33\) a year.

Hint

For (c), think about GPs already in post, about which GPs would leave or retire early, and about how the contract was agreed.

Exercise 3.10 (Case)

In Example 3.2, raise \(\mul\) to \(0.5\) so that \(\Dpi\) halves. Compute the new second-best contract, \(w_b^*\), and the welfare loss. Is the high action still worth inducing? What kind of medical work does a small \(\Dpi\) describe?

Hint

Now \(c/\Dpi=2\).

Exercise 3.11 (Case)

QOF paid on a ratio: the number of patients reaching a target divided by the number of eligible patients, where the practice could exclude patients it judged unsuitable.

  • (a) Explain why the decision to exclude a patient is a second hidden action, and how it changes what the observed ratio tells the NHS about proactive care.

  • (b) Which assumption of The model does this violate?

  • (c) Suggest two changes to the contract or to monitoring that would reduce the problem, and say what each costs.

Hint

Ask who keeps the records on which the payment is based, and whether the likelihood of a “good” ratio still depends only on proactive care.

Exercise 3.12 (Case)

In Example 3.3:

  • (a) Check that under the partial-cover contract the driver is indifferent between careful and careless driving, and between buying the policy and not.

  • (b) Show that, when care is hidden, no full-cover policy makes money.

Hint

For (b): what action does a fully insured driver choose, and what is the highest premium he will accept?

Exercise 3.13 (Case)

Drivers are 30% safer while monitored and less safe afterwards. Which two results in this chapter does that one fact illustrate? Hard braking falls by about 21% under monitoring but mileage does not change. What does that tell you about which action was hidden?

Hint

Which component of driving does the deductible already reward, and which one does the device reveal?

3.8.3. Advanced#

Exercise 3.14 (Advanced)

Let \(n=3\), \(p^H=(0.2,0.4,0.4)\), \(p^L=(0.4,0.2,0.4)\). Show that MLRP fails and that the optimal contract satisfies \(w_2^*>w_3^*>w_1^*\). Explain intuitively why the highest output is not the best-paid one.

Hint

Rank the outcomes by \(1-\ell_i\) and use (3.8).

Exercise 3.15 (Advanced)

Extend Example 3.2 with a signal \(s\in\{0,1\}\). Given output, \(\Pr(s=1\mid y,\ah)=0.8\) and \(\Pr(s=1\mid y,\al)=0.4\), independent of \(y\).

  • (a) Compute the four likelihood ratios \(\ell(y,s)\) and rank the outcomes by optimal pay. Is pay monotone in output?

  • (b) Using Proposition 3.3, compute the agency cost with and without the signal.

  • (c) What happens if instead \(\Pr(s=1\mid y,a)=0.8\) for both actions?

Hint

Joint probabilities multiply: \(p^a(y,s)=p^a(y)\Pr(s\mid y,a)\).

Exercise 3.16 (Advanced)

In Proposition 3.4, suppose the floor is set by the principal’s own choice of a “guarantee” \(\underline z\ge\uo-\mul c/\Dpi\). Show that her profit is strictly decreasing in \(\underline z\). Then give one reason, outside the model, why a principal such as the NHS might still choose \(\underline z=\uo\).

Hint

Write the expected wage bill as a function of \(\underline z\).

Exercise 3.17 (Advanced)

Show by example that in the \(n\)-outcome model the second-best contract can be strictly costlier than the first best even when the agent is risk-neutral, provided there is a limited-liability constraint \(w_i\ge0\).

Hint

Take \(u(w)=w\), \(\uo=0\), two outcomes, and compare with Proposition 3.4.
