Welfare

5.6. Welfare#

Firms earn zero profit in every equilibrium, so total surplus is the worker’s expected payoff:

\[ SW=\bar\theta-\mu_0c(a^*(\theta_h);\theta_h)-(1-\mu_0)c(a^*(\theta_l);\theta_l). \]

Output is \(\bar\theta\) whatever happens, so every pound spent on the signal is lost.

Proposition 5.1 (Signalling is socially wasteful)

Every separating equilibrium, and every pooling equilibrium with \(a_p>0\), has strictly lower total surplus than the benchmarks of Benchmarks.

Proof of Proposition 5.1

In both benchmarks \(SW=\bar\theta\). In a separating equilibrium \(SW=\bar\theta-\mu_0c(a_h^*;\theta_h)\) with \(a_h^*\ge\underline a>0\); in a pooling one, \(SW=\bar\theta-\mu_0c(a_p;\theta_h)-(1-\mu_0)c(a_p;\theta_l)\). Both cost terms are strictly positive.

Proposition 5.2 (Who gains from separation)

Compared with the no-signal outcome, the low type is strictly worse off in every separating equilibrium. The high type is better off in the least-cost separating equilibrium if and only if \(\theta_h-c(\underline a;\theta_h)\ge\bar\theta\).

Proof of Proposition 5.2

The low type gets \(\theta_l<\bar\theta\). The high type gets \(\theta_h-c(\underline a;\theta_h)\) against \(\bar\theta\).

Interpretation. In the example the high type ends up exactly where she started, \(29\), and the low type loses \(9\). If more than half of workers were high types, \(\bar\theta\) would exceed \(29\) and the high type would be strictly worse off with signalling, yet she would still signal. She cannot choose the no-signal outcome on her own: if she alone stopped studying, firms would take her for a low type and pay \(20\). Signalling is an arms race that individuals cannot opt out of. A ban on the signal (or a rule that makes it uninformative) would raise total surplus; whether it is a Pareto improvement depends on \(\mu_0\).
