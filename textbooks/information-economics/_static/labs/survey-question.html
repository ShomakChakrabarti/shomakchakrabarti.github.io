Which question would you ask?

The government wants to know whether a 20% higher price will reduce smoking. You are writing the survey. Click the question you would put in it.

You chose A. It is the natural question, but its answer mixes the price with everything else that changes next year. The surgeon answers “more” because difficult operations are coming up, and a survey of answers like hers would conclude that the tax does nothing. Question B is the one that isolates the price.
You chose B. It holds income and workload fixed, so the answer isolates the one factor the policy is about. That is exactly what a model does: it shuts down the factors the question is not about. It does not claim they are unimportant.
