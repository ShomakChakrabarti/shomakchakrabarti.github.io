4.9. Exercises#

4.9.1. Core#

Exercise 4.1 (Core)

Let \(\theta\) be uniform on \([0,1]\).

  • (a) Show that \(\E[\theta\mid\theta\le p]=p/2\) for \(p\in(0,1]\) and that \(\E[\theta\mid a\le\theta\le p]=(a+p)/2\) for \(0\le a<p\le1\).

  • (b) Now let \(\theta\) be uniform on \([L,H]\). What is \(\E[\theta\mid\theta\le p]\) for \(L<p\le H\)?

Hint

A uniform distribution truncated to an interval is uniform on that interval. Or compute \(\int\theta f(\theta)\,d\theta/\int f(\theta)\,d\theta\).

Exercise 4.2 (Core)

Prove Proposition 4.2 in full, using Definition 4.3. Then explain in two sentences why the seller’s decision to sell carries no information.

Hint

Ask what a seller knows when he decides whether to sell.

Exercise 4.3 (Core)

Solve the asymmetric-information model when \(k=2\) and when \(k=2.5\). In each case, which units trade and at what price? Draw \(\varphi\) and the \(45^\circ\) line.

Hint

Use Proposition 4.3. For \(k=2\), which prices in \([0,1]\) are acceptable to buyers?

Exercise 4.4 (Core)

Suppose a seller’s outside option is \(\alpha\theta\) with \(\alpha\in(0,1]\), while everything else is as in Theorem 4.1.

  • (a) Which units are offered at price \(p\)?

  • (b) Show that the market unravels completely if and only if \(k<2\alpha\).

  • (c) Interpret \(\alpha\). Compute the equilibrium for \(k=1.5\) and \(\alpha=0.5\).

Hint

At price \(p\), the seller sells if \(\alpha\theta\le p\). What is the largest quality offered?

Exercise 4.5 (Core)

In Proposition 4.5, let \(k=1.5\).

  • (a) For \(r=0.3\), compute the market’s surplus and the surplus a planner who observes \(\theta\) could achieve.

  • (b) Show that in general the gap is \(r^2/(2k)\) when \(r\le k/2\), and explain which units are traded that should not be.

Hint

The informed planner trades a unit if and only if \(k\theta\ge r\).

Exercise 4.6 (Core)

In the spiral of Proposition 4.4 with \(k=1.5\) and \(p_0=0.75\), after how many further rounds does the price first fall below \(0.05\)?

Hint

\(p_t=0.75^{\,t}\times0.75\). Take logarithms.

Exercise 4.7 (Core)

Suppose there are only two qualities, \(\theta_L=0.2\) and \(\theta_H=0.8\), each owned by half the sellers. Sellers’ outside option is \(\theta\) and buyers value quality \(\theta\) at \(k\theta\).

  • (a) For \(k=1.5\), find the equilibrium. Which type trades?

  • (b) Find the smallest \(k\) for which both types trade.

  • (c) Compute the lost surplus in (a).

Hint

Check two candidate prices: one at which only low types sell, and one at which both do.

Exercise 4.8 (Core)

A seller of a high-quality car offers to sign a statement that the car is of high quality, with no legal consequences if it is false. Explain why this does not change the equilibrium. What would have to be true of the statement for it to help?

Hint

Would a seller of a low-quality car sign the same statement?

4.9.2. Cases#

Exercise 4.9 (Case)

In Example 4.2, why does the price fall by exactly a quarter each round? What happens if \(k=2\)? If \(k=2.5\)?

Hint

The ratio of successive prices is \(\varphi(p)/p\).

Exercise 4.10 (Case)

  • (a) Derive \(p^*\) with a quality floor \(a\), and explain why \(a\ge1/3\) is enough for full trade when \(k=1.5\).

  • (b) With \(a=0.2\), what share of the efficient surplus is recovered?

  • (c) An MOT certifies a minimum standard on one day. Is that Proposition 4.1, or a quality floor?

Hint

Surplus from trading qualities in \([a,p^*]\) is \(\int_a^{p^*}(k-1)\theta\,d\theta\).

Exercise 4.11 (Case)

Peterson and Schneider find adverse selection for gearboxes but not for bodywork. Which two results in this chapter does that one finding illustrate? Does CarMax’s success depend on being a large repeat seller?

Hint

One result is about public quality, one about private quality. For the second part, think about reputation.

Exercise 4.12 (Case)

Use Example 4.3.

  • (a) Translate the spiral of Proposition 4.4 into this market, line by line.

  • (b) Why does the plan lose money in year 1, when the premium equals the previous average cost?

  • (c) Derive the long-run share enrolled, \((2k-2)/(2k-1)\).

  • (d) With an 85% employer subsidy, show that about 94% stay enrolled.

Hint

For (c), find the fixed point of \(P=1-\hat\theta/2\) with \(\hat\theta=1-P/k\).

Exercise 4.13 (Case)

Harvard saved 5–8% on premiums and lost 2–4% of spending to adverse selection. Was the change a mistake? And since the NHS equalises outside options in the UK, why does private medical insurance in the UK not unravel in the same way?

Hint

Who bears each of the two numbers? For the second part, think about who buys private cover and how it is priced.

4.9.3. Advanced#

Exercise 4.14 (Advanced)

Let \(F(\theta)=\theta^\beta\) on \([0,1]\) with \(\beta>0\), \(s(\theta)=\theta\) and \(b(\theta)=k\theta\).

  • (a) Show that \(\E[\theta\mid\theta\le p]=\beta p/(\beta+1)\).

  • (b) Show that the market unravels completely if and only if \(k<(\beta+1)/\beta\).

  • (c) Interpret: what does a large \(\beta\) mean, and why does it make unravelling harder?

Hint

The density is \(\beta\theta^{\beta-1}\).

Exercise 4.15 (Advanced)

In Exercise 4.7, let \(k=1.8\). Show that there are two prices at which buyers pay exactly their expected value, and that the higher one Pareto-dominates. Which does Definition 4.3 select?

Hint

Consider \(p=k\theta_L\) and \(p=k(\theta_L+\theta_H)/2\).

Exercise 4.16 (Advanced)

In Proposition 4.9, take \(k=1.5\). What share of the efficient surplus is recovered with \(n=2\), \(4\) and \(10\) grades? Why does the proposition need \(k\ge4/3\), and what happens to the second grade when \(k=1.2\)?

Hint

Lost surplus is \((k-1)w^2/2\); efficient surplus is \((k-1)/2\).

Exercise 4.17 (Advanced)

In the demand–cost-curve framework, suppose \(P(q)=1.6-q\), \(MC(q)=1.5-q\) and \(AC(q)=1.5-q/2\) on \([0,1]\).

  • (a) Find equilibrium and efficient coverage, and the welfare loss.

  • (b) A mandate requires everyone to buy. What is the zero-profit price? Who gains and who loses relative to the equilibrium?

Hint

Equilibrium solves \(P(q)=AC(q)\).
