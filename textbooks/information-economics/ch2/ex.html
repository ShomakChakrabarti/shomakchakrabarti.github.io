2.8. Exercises#

2.8.1. Core#

Exercise 2.1 (Core)

Let \(u(x)=\sqrt x\).

  • (a) Find the certainty equivalent and risk premium of \(\{0\circ\frac12;\ 100\circ\frac12\}\).

  • (b) Find the certainty equivalent of the lottery in Example 2.2, and confirm the risk premium of \(3\).

Hint

Compute expected utility first, then invert \(u\).

Exercise 2.2 (Core)

Show directly that \(u(x)=\sqrt x\) and \(v(x)=e^2\sqrt x+12\) rank the lotteries of Example 2.3 the same way, and explain why \(x^2\) does not.

Hint

Use Proposition 2.2. Is \(x^2\) an affine function of \(\sqrt x\)?

Exercise 2.3 (Core)

Show that if \(\succsim\) has an expected utility representation, then \(\pi\sim\rho\) implies \(\pi\sim w\pi+(1-w)\rho\) for every \(w\). Interpret this in the probability triangle.

Hint

Expected utility is linear in probabilities (T1).

Exercise 2.4 (Core)

Prove that a decision maker is risk loving if and only if \(u\) is strictly convex. Give an example of a lottery that a risk lover would pay to play even though its expected value is below the price.

Hint

Copy the proof of Proposition 2.3 with the inequalities reversed.

Exercise 2.5 (Core)

Let \(u(x)=\frac1\theta(1-e^{-\theta x})\) with \(\theta=0.01\).

  • (a) Show that \(\lambda(x)=\theta\) for all \(x\).

  • (b) Show that the risk premium of the 50–50 lottery \(x\pm h\) is \(\frac1\theta\ln\cosh(\theta h)\), whatever \(x\). Compute it for \(h=100\).

  • (c) Why does a CARA decision maker’s risk premium not depend on wealth?

Hint

\(\frac12e^{-\theta(x+h)}+\frac12e^{-\theta(x-h)}=e^{-\theta x}\cosh(\theta h)\).

Exercise 2.6 (Core)

For \(u(x)=x^{1-\gamma}/(1-\gamma)\), compute \(\lambda(x)\) and \(\mu(x)\). Show that the risk premium of a lottery that multiplies wealth by \(1\pm\delta\) with equal probabilities is proportional to wealth.

Hint

Write the certainty equivalent as \(x\) times a number that does not depend on \(x\).

Exercise 2.7 (Core)

In Example 2.2, what is the largest premium the decision maker would pay for full insurance? Split it into the expected loss and the risk premium.

Hint

She is indifferent when her sure wealth equals the certainty equivalent of the uninsured lottery.

Exercise 2.8 (Core)

Use Example 2.5.

  • (a) Solve the first-order condition for \(c^*\) when \(\gamma=0.3\).

  • (b) Find the price \(\gamma\) above which she buys no cover at all.

  • (c) Explain why the decision maker never buys more than full cover, whatever the price.

Hint

For (b), set \(V'(0)=0\). For (c), sign \(V'(c)\) for \(c>l\).

2.8.2. Cases#

Exercise 2.9 (Case)

In the Allais choices, show that a decision maker who chooses \(B\) and \(C\) cannot be an expected-utility maximiser. Then show that she would also violate independence: write \(A,B,C,D\) as mixtures with a common component.

Hint

\(A\) and \(B\) share a \(0.66\) chance of \(2400\); \(C\) and \(D\) share a \(0.66\) chance of \(0\).

Exercise 2.10 (Case)

Kahneman and Tversky also offered: \(E\): \(4000\) with probability \(0.8\), or \(F\): \(3000\) for sure; and \(G\): \(4000\) with probability \(0.2\), or \(H\): \(3000\) with probability \(0.25\). Most chose \(F\) and \(G\). Show that this pattern violates expected utility. What do the second pair’s probabilities have in common with the first’s?

Hint

\(G\) and \(H\) are \(E\) and \(F\) played with probability \(\frac14\).

Exercise 2.11 (Case)

Use Sydnor’s numbers: a $100 extra premium for a $500 lower deductible, claim probability \(0.043\).

  • (a) What is the expected payout of the lower deductible? By what percentage does the premium exceed it?

  • (b) By Proposition 2.6, should a risk-averse expected-utility maximiser choose the lower deductible? What does the answer depend on?

  • (c) Verify the claim in Example 2.6 that \(\theta\ge0.006\) is needed.

Hint

For (c), evaluate both sides of the inequality at \(\theta=0.006\).

Exercise 2.12 (Case)

A phone costs £900 to replace and the chance of breaking it in a year is \(10\%\). Insurance costs £8 a month. What loading does this imply? A buyer has \(u=\ln x\) and wealth £3,000. Should she buy it? What would your answer be for wealth £1,000?

Hint

Compare the expected utility with and without insurance; the annual premium is £96.

2.8.3. Advanced#

Exercise 2.13 (Advanced)

Show that if \(v\) is more risk averse than \(u\) (in the sense of Theorem 2.2), then at any loaded price \(v\) buys at least as much cover as \(u\).

Hint

Compare the two first-order conditions at \(u\)’s optimum, using \(v'=g'(u)\,u'\) with \(g'\) decreasing.

Exercise 2.14 (Advanced)

In Example 2.2, compute the Arrow–Pratt approximation of the risk premium and compare it with the exact value. Repeat for the lottery \(\{80\circ\frac12;\ 88\circ\frac12\}\). Why is the second approximation better?

Hint

\(\lambda(x)=1/(2x)\) for \(u=\sqrt x\).

Exercise 2.15 (Advanced)

  • (a) Show that \(\{0\circ\frac14;\ 50\circ\frac12;\ 100\circ\frac14\}\) is a mean-preserving spread of \(\delta_{50}\) and of \(\{25\circ\frac12;\ 75\circ\frac12\}\).

  • (b) Let \(P=\{10\circ0.9;\ 100\circ0.1\}\) and \(Q=\{1\circ0.1;\ 21\circ0.9\}\). Show that they have the same mean, that \(Q\) has the smaller variance, and that neither is a mean-preserving spread of the other.

Hint

For (b), find one concave \(u\) that prefers \(Q\) and one that prefers \(P\). Try \(u(x)=\sqrt x\) and \(u(x)=\min\{x,10\}\).

Exercise 2.16 (Advanced)

Consider rank-dependent utility with \(u(x)=x\) and a weighting function with \(q(0.33)=0.33\), \(q(0.34)=0.335\), \(q(0.99)=0.9\) and \(q(1)=1\). Show that this decision maker chooses \(B\) over \(A\) and \(C\) over \(D\) in the Allais choices. Which feature of \(q\) produces each choice?

Hint

For \(A\) the decision weights are \(q(0.33)\) on \(2500\), \(q(0.99)-q(0.33)\) on \(2400\) and \(1-q(0.99)\) on \(0\).
