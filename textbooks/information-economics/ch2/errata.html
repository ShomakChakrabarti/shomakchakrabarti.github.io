2.9. Corrections relative to the lecture slides#

Corrections to the lecture slides

The following changes were made relative to the Choice under uncertainty slides. Slide numbers refer to that deck.

  1. Slide 3 (Setup). The second half of the slide is copied from the hidden-action deck (“the principal faces a situation of uncertainty”, “the principal wants to hire the agent”). The setup here has a single decision maker and no principal or agent.

  2. Slide 7 (weather). With sun preferred to rain, a sensible decision maker prefers the 20% chance of rain (\(\rho\)) to the 60% chance (\(\pi\)), not the reverse. The point of the slide, that a preferred lottery can still produce the worse outcome, is kept in Example 2.1.

  3. Slides 9–10, 13 (axioms). Theorem 1 assumes a “continuous” preference without stating the axiom. Continuity is stated here as Assumption 2.3. Independence is stated as an “if and only if” for \(w\in(0,1]\); with \(w=0\) the slide’s version is empty, and the converse direction is used in the proof.

  4. Slide 20. The same result is labelled Proposition 1 and Proposition 2 on consecutive slides, and both risk aversion (slide 28) and insurance demand (slide 46) are also called Proposition 1. Here they are Proposition 2.2, Proposition 2.3, Proposition 2.5.

  5. Slide 26 (risk aversion). The definition must be for non-degenerate lotteries: a degenerate lottery with mean \(\bar x\) is \(\delta_{\bar x}\) itself, which cannot be strictly preferred to itself.

  6. Slide 24. The certainty equivalent is described both as the least the decision maker would accept for the lottery and as what she would pay for it. These are the same number only if she owns the lottery; Definition 2.4 uses the selling interpretation, and the risk premium the insurance one.

  7. Slide 31. “Risk aversion if \(\lambda(x)\) is positive” should read “if \(\lambda(x)>0\) for all \(x\)”; the converse can fail at isolated points (Proposition 2.4).

  8. Slide 32. “Since \(u\) is invariant to scaling (Proposition 1)” refers to the affine-uniqueness result, not to Proposition 1 on risk aversion.

  9. Slides 47–48 (insurance). The notation changes from \((y_g,y_b)\) to wealth \(x\) and loss \(l\) without warning. In the first-order condition, the step from \(u'(G)=u'(B)\) to \(G=B\) uses that \(u'\) is strictly decreasing (strict concavity), not strictly increasing. Footnote 2’s Kuhn–Tucker remark is replaced by signing \(V'\) directly (Proposition 2.5).

  10. Placeholders. “(see Slide xx)” on slides 10, 16, 20, 23; blank “Broader literature”, “Intuition behind risk aversion”, “Expected utility: illustration”, “Full insurance in observed markets” and “Appendix” slides; “Issue with absolute risk aversion” contains placeholder text; a stray “lkjl” on slide 3. The relevant material is in Attitudes towards risk and The theory meets the data.

  11. Typos. “ot mitigate” (slide 22); “predections” (slide 34); “as \(\lambda_u(x)\) decreases” (slide 35); “Chakrbarti” on the title slide.
