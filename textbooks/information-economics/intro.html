Information Economics#

Hidden information, hidden actions, and how the proofs work. Shomak Chakrabarti, University of Manchester. Draft, October 2026.

Important

Please read Before you start first. Uploading these materials to ChatGPT or any other AI tool, including for summaries, is not permitted on this course.

How to use this book#

Each chapter develops one model from the lectures, proves every result in full, and then takes the model’s predictions to real markets.

Proofs open on demand. Every proof is folded away under its theorem. Before opening it, try to see how you would prove the result yourself. Where a proof has a Proof strategy box, open that first: it says what the argument is going to do and why that route works. Inside a proof, Why this step? notes explain the purpose of individual steps.

Proof toolkit boxes isolate techniques that recur across the book (perturbation arguments, Jensen’s inequality, relaxing a constraint), so that you learn to recognise and reuse them.

Exercises are tagged Core, Case or Advanced. Hints are folded under each exercise; full solutions are held by your instructor.

Advanced sections generalise the lecture model for research students and can be skipped on a first reading.

Note

This edition is generated automatically from the LaTeX source of the printed book, so the two always say the same thing. The book has four parts, each opening with an introduction. Part 0, economic models, is Chapter 0: what a model is, how the models in this book are built, and how to criticise one; start there. Part I, choice over time, is Chapter 1, on factor markets: saving, investment and net present value. Part II, choice under uncertainty, is Chapter 2. Part III, asymmetric information, comprises Chapter 3, hidden actions (moral hazard), and Chapters 4 and 5, hidden information (adverse selection and signalling).
